Problem

A trading desk needs a defensible FRTB standardised market-risk capital number by risk class and bucket, both as a live charge and as the output floor against internal models.

Approach

  1. Ingest standardised sensitivities (GIRR, CSR, equity, FX, commodity) for a multi-asset book in a clean, CRIF-like format.
  2. Implement the sensitivities-based method: apply risk weights, bucket, and aggregate within and across buckets under the high/medium/low correlation scenarios.
  3. Add the default risk charge (jump-to-default) and the residual risk add-on for exotic/residual exposures.
  4. Aggregate to total SA capital and decompose the charge by risk class, bucket and component.
  5. Expose it as an interactive tool where changing a position or sensitivity updates capital and shows the binding correlation scenario.

Deliverables & metrics

  • FRTB-SA capital engine with SBM, DRC and RRAO
  • Interactive capital dashboard with risk-class/bucket drill-down
  • Methodology note mapping the calc to MAS Notice 637 / BCBS FRTB

Show in interview

  • Total FRTB-SA capital by risk class
  • SBM vs. DRC vs. RRAO contribution
  • Binding correlation scenario (high/medium/low)
  • Capital impact of a hedge or new trade

Stack: Python · pandas / numpy · Next.js / interactive charts · CRIF-style data

Sample talk track

I decomposed the SA charge and showed that curvature and the credit-spread bucket drove most of the capital, and that the low-correlation scenario was binding — so a diversifying hedge that looked cheap on VaR actually cut FRTB capital more than expected. That's the kind of capital-aware insight the desk needs before it trades.

PRD

Product requirements

Problem

Under the Basel III finalisation (FRTB), the desk must compute market risk capital using the Standardised Approach (SA) as both a fallback for desks that fail the Internal Models Approach (IMA) eligibility tests (P&L attribution and backtesting) and as a mandatory floor/reporting figure. Legacy VaR-based standardised charges are non-compliant and cannot decompose capital into the Sensitivities-Based Method (SBM), Default Risk Charge (DRC) and Residual Risk Add-On (RRAO), leaving Product Control unable to reconcile, explain drivers, or optimise capital consumption per desk.

Objective

Deliver a transparent, auditable FRTB-SA engine that ingests regulatory delta/vega/curvature sensitivities, applies prescribed risk weights and correlations across the three correlation scenarios (high/medium/low), aggregates SBM by risk class and bucket, adds DRC and RRAO, and produces the final RWA (capital x 12.5) with full drill-down for daily capital reporting and desk-level attribution.

Users

Market Risk capital analysts computing daily/monthly FRTB-SA chargesProduct Control validating capital movements against P&L and inventoryFront-office desk heads managing capital consumption and RWA budgetsRegulatory reporting team submitting COREP/MAS 637 market risk returnsModel validation and internal audit reviewing methodology compliance

Data inputs

  • Regulatory sensitivities (CRIF-style delta/vega per risk factor and curvature scenario P&L vectors) from the pricing/risk engine.
  • Prescribed risk weights, bucket definitions and correlation matrices (rho, gamma) from the regulatory parameter library.
  • Jump-to-default (JTD) exposures, notionals, seniority and rating for the DRC computation.
  • Trade and position inventory with product taxonomy flags for RRAO eligibility (exotic underlying, gap risk).
  • FX spot rates for translating charges into the reporting currency.
  • Desk hierarchy and book mapping for capital allocation and RWA budgeting.

Functional requirements

  • Compute SBM delta, vega and curvature charges for all seven risk classes (GIRR, CSR non-securitised, CSR securitised, CSR correlation trading, Equity, Commodity, FX) using prescribed risk weights and intra/inter-bucket correlations.
  • Run the three correlation scenarios (rho/gamma scaled to high, medium, low) and select the maximum aggregate charge per the FRTB-SA rules.
  • Calculate the curvature charge via the prescribed upward/downward shock of underlying prices, netting against delta and applying the CVR (curvature risk) aggregation.
  • Compute the Default Risk Charge (DRC) with gross JTD long/short offsetting within buckets, prescribed default risk weights, and the hedge benefit ratio (WtS).
  • Apply the Residual Risk Add-On (RRAO) as a notional-based charge for exotic underlyings and other residual risks (gap, correlation, behavioural).
  • Aggregate SBM + DRC + RRAO to total market risk capital and gross up to RWA using the 12.5 multiplier, with desk, bucket and risk-factor drill-down.
  • Produce day-on-day capital attribution decomposing moves into sensitivity changes, new/matured trades, market data (risk weight/correlation) and FX translation.

Non-functional

  • Full auditability: every capital number reproducible to source sensitivity, risk weight and correlation with a versioned regulatory parameter set.
  • Batch completion for the full trading book within the overnight window (target < 60 minutes) to meet T+1 capital reporting.
  • Deterministic and idempotent computation so re-runs on identical inputs yield identical capital to the cent.
  • Configurable regulatory calendar and parameter governance to absorb Basel/MAS rule recalibrations without code change.

Success metrics

  • 100% reconciliation of engine SBM/DRC/RRAO totals to the regulatory return within tolerance (< 0.1%).
  • Day-on-day capital variance explained to > 95% by the attribution engine.
  • Overnight batch SLA met on > 99% of business days.
  • Reduction in manual reconciliation effort by > 50% versus the legacy spreadsheet process.
  • Zero unexplained methodology exceptions raised in model validation review.
SOP

Standard operating procedure

Purpose

Run the overnight FRTB-SA capital batch: ingest regulatory sensitivities, aggregate SBM/DRC/RRAO, compare SA capital to the IMA floor, and publish auditable RWA for T+1 reporting.

Frequency

Daily overnight capital batch (T+1); formal month-end parameter lock and regulatory close.

1

Confirm delta/vega/curvature sensitivity, JTD and RRAO-flagged inventory feeds are complete versus the trading-book golden source before the overnight batch starts.

Owner: Market Risk Analyst

Control: Row-count and desk-coverage reconciliation; batch auto-held if completeness variance exceeds threshold.

2

Lock the approved FRTB risk-weight, correlation and DRC parameter pack (version and effective date) from the regulatory parameter library.

Owner: Risk Methodology

Control: Four-eyes parameter sign-off with hash match to the governance register.

3

Compute SBM delta, vega and curvature by risk class and bucket under the three correlation scenarios; select the maximum aggregate SBM charge.

Owner: Market Risk Analyst

Control: Automated scenario-selection assertion per risk class; run log retained with input snapshot ID.

4

Calculate DRC (JTD offsetting and WtS) and RRAO (notional add-on), then sum SBM + DRC + RRAO to SA market-risk capital and RWA (×12.5).

Owner: Market Risk Analyst

Control: Component roll-forward check; capital equals sum of SBM, DRC and RRAO within rounding tolerance.

5

Compare SA capital to the IMA output and the output floor; flag desks where SA binds or the floor lifts reported capital.

Owner: Market Risk Capital Lead

Control: Floor-binding exception report with desk-level commentary mandatory above materiality.

6

Attribute day-on-day capital moves to sensitivity, new/matured trades, parameter and FX drivers; reconcile totals to Product Control.

Owner: Product Control

Control: Independent inventory/P&L reconciliation with signed break log and aging.

7

Publish approved SA capital and RWA to the regulatory reporting layer for COREP / MAS 637.

Owner: Regulatory Reporting

Control: Maker-checker on submission keyed to the approved run ID.

Escalation

Failed sensitivity feeds, unexplained capital moves above materiality, floor-binding surprises, or reconciliation breaks unresolved before the reporting cut-off escalate to Head of Market Risk and Head of Product Control; regulatory-return impacts escalate to the CRO with a written remediation plan.
