Ex-Ante Tracking Error & Active Risk Decomposition

“I decomposed a benchmark-relative fund's tracking error into the active bets actually driving it — and flagged an unintended sector tilt the PM didn't know they had.”

Tracking Error & Active Share

Problem

A benchmark-relative mandate must stay within an ex-ante tracking-error budget. Risk must measure active risk vs the benchmark, decompose it into sources, and monitor active share and unintended bets.

Approach

  1. Construct active weights (portfolio minus benchmark) and map to a common factor/risk model.
  2. Compute ex-ante tracking error and decompose into factor, sector, country and stock-specific contributions.
  3. Calculate active share and identify unintended vs intended active bets.
  4. Set a tracking-error limit with utilisation and alerts, and attribute day-over-day TE moves.
  5. Reconcile ex-ante TE against realised TE to validate the risk model.

Deliverables & metrics

  • Tracking-error decomposition by source
  • Active share and unintended-bet monitor
  • Ex-ante vs realised TE validation

Show in interview

  • Ex-ante tracking error vs budget
  • TE contribution by factor / sector / stock-specific
  • Active share %
  • Ex-ante vs realised TE gap

Stack: Python · pandas / numpy · Factor/risk model · Plotly / dashboard

Sample talk track

Ex-ante TE was 3.1% against a 3.5% budget — fine on the surface. But the decomposition showed a third of it came from an unintended overweight to rate-sensitive sectors, a by-product of stock selection. Surfacing that let the PM neutralise the tilt and redeploy the risk budget into their actual convictions.

PRD

Product requirements

Problem

Active managers are paid for intentional bets versus a benchmark, but portfolios accumulate unintended exposures that consume the risk budget without conviction. Without ex-ante tracking-error and active-risk decomposition, the desk cannot see how much active risk exists, where it comes from (factor vs specific), whether active share justifies fees, or which positions are unintended bets.

Objective

Deliver an ex-ante tracking-error and active-risk decomposition tool that computes forecast tracking error from active weights and a risk model, decomposes active risk into factor and stock-specific components, measures active share, and surfaces unintended exposures so PMs can align risk with conviction.

Users

Active equity / multi-asset portfolio managersRisk managers monitoring active risk budgetsPerformance and attribution analystsInvestment director / CIOClient reporting and product teams

Data inputs

  • Portfolio holdings and weights
  • Benchmark constituents and weights
  • Multi-factor risk model: factor exposures, covariance and specific risk
  • Security master and classification (sector/industry/country/currency)
  • Mandate parameters: target tracking-error band and constraints
  • Investment thesis / intended-exposure tags per position

Functional requirements

  • Compute active weights (portfolio minus benchmark) and forecast ex-ante tracking error using a multi-factor risk model covariance matrix.
  • Decompose active risk into factor risk (style, industry, country, currency) and stock-specific risk, with each contribution reconciling to total tracking error.
  • Calculate active share and compare it to tracking error to characterise the strategy (concentrated stock-picker vs factor-tilted vs closet-indexer).
  • Identify unintended bets: material active factor or sector exposures inconsistent with the stated investment thesis, ranked by risk contribution.
  • Support what-if analysis so a PM can test how a trade changes tracking error, active share and factor exposures before dealing.
  • Track active risk versus the mandated tracking-error band and flag when the portfolio drifts outside the target range.
  • Produce an active-risk report with marginal contribution to tracking error by position, factor and sector.

Non-functional

  • Return ex-ante tracking error and what-if results interactively (target < 5 seconds) to support pre-trade decisions.
  • Consistency with the firm-standard risk model and benchmark constituents to keep results explainable and comparable to attribution.
  • Reproducibility of every ex-ante calculation from pinned holdings, benchmark and risk-model snapshots.
  • Clear documentation of risk-model assumptions for CIO and client-facing use.

Success metrics

  • Ex-ante tracking error kept within the mandated band on ≥ 95% of observation days
  • Active-risk decomposition reconciles to total tracking error within tolerance
  • Reduction in unintended factor risk contribution over time as bets are cleaned up
  • Active share consistent with the fee/strategy positioning agreed with clients
  • What-if tool used ahead of the majority of risk-material trades
SOP

Standard operating procedure

Purpose

Measure ex-ante tracking error against the mandate band, check active share for closet-indexing or over-concentration, and clear unintended bets so active risk matches stated conviction.

Frequency

Daily ex-ante TE vs mandate band; pre-trade what-if on material tickets; monthly active-share and unintended-bet review with the PM and investment director.

1

Align portfolio and benchmark as-of dates, compute active weights, and forecast ex-ante tracking error from the firm risk model.

Owner: Risk analyst

Control: Benchmark vendor reconciliation; risk-model version pinned; large day-over-day TE moves require commentary.

2

Compare forecast TE to the mandated lower and upper band; classify as within appetite, approaching edge, or breached.

Owner: Risk analyst

Control: System band thresholds feed the risk register; both high-TE and low-TE (closet) breaches are in scope.

3

Compute active share and characterise the book (stock-picker vs factor-tilt vs closet-indexer) against the product's fee and client positioning.

Owner: Risk analyst

Control: Active-share floor/ceiling from the product mandate; trend alert on sustained drift.

4

Decompose TE into factor vs specific risk and flag unintended factor/sector bets inconsistent with the thesis.

Owner: Senior risk analyst with PM

Control: Decomposition reconciles to total TE; unintended-exposure watchlist reviewed with the PM.

5

Run mandate check: TE band, active-share bounds, and any hard active-weight or sector constraints; agree remediation or temporary exception.

Owner: Risk manager with investment director

Control: Breach or exception logged with owner and close date; client-facing products may need product-board notice.

6

Publish the active-risk report; keep what-if TE available so the PM can test trades before dealing.

Owner: Risk manager

Control: Signed pack with commentary; what-if uses the same pinned risk-model snapshot.

Escalation

TE drifting toward the band edge or rising unintended exposures escalate to the PM for repositioning. A TE band breach (too high or closet-indexing too low), active-share outside mandate, or an unintended factor bet above its contribution limit escalates to the investment director/CIO with a dated remediation plan.
