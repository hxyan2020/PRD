Trading Limit Monitoring & Breach-Triage Workflow

“I built the limit-monitoring and breach-triage workflow a traded-risk team lives on — VaR, sensitivity and stress limits with utilisation, breach classification and escalation.”

Problem

A bank's trading desks operate under a hierarchy of VaR/ES, sensitivity, stress-loss and concentration limits. Risk must monitor utilisation, classify breaches and escalate consistently with clear evidence.

Approach

  1. Model a limit tree by desk and risk factor with soft (warning) and hard limits derived from risk appetite.
  2. Compute utilisation across VaR/ES, DV01/CS01/vega, stress-loss and concentration metrics.
  3. Classify breaches (active vs. passive vs. technical) and apply escalation and remediation SLAs.
  4. Generate an auditable breach log with cause, action and sign-off, and a management dashboard of utilisation and breaches.
  5. Reconcile limit consumption against capital and stress appetite so limits stay economically meaningful.

Deliverables & metrics

  • Limit-utilisation and breach dashboard
  • Breach classification and escalation workflow
  • Auditable breach log with remediation sign-off

Show in interview

  • Limit utilisation % by desk and metric
  • Breach count by type (active / passive / technical)
  • Time-to-remediation vs. SLA
  • Utilisation vs. capital / stress appetite

Stack: Python · pandas · SQL · Dashboard / reporting

Sample talk track

The dashboard surfaced a desk creeping to 95% VaR utilisation while its stress-loss limit was already amber — a passive breach driven by a market move, not new risk. The workflow escalated it with the cause and a remediation plan attached, so the committee saw not just that a limit broke but why and what was done, which is exactly what good breach governance looks like.

PRD

Product requirements

Problem

Market and counterparty risk limits (VaR, sensitivity/Greeks, notional, PFE, stress and concentration) generate a high volume of daily breaches, but analysts cannot quickly distinguish active (trader-driven) from passive (market-move or limit-change driven) breaches, attribute the driver, or route escalation per the limit hierarchy. This delays remediation, risks missing genuine limit excesses, and creates governance findings around untimely escalation and sign-off.

Objective

Provide a limit breach triage workflow that consolidates breaches across the limit hierarchy, classifies active vs. passive, attributes breach drivers, prioritises by severity and limit tier, and enforces the escalation and sign-off chain with a full audit trail.

Users

Market Risk analysts triaging and clearing daily breachesCounterparty credit risk analysts managing PFE/limit excessesDesk heads acknowledging and remediating active breachesRisk managers and CRO office monitoring limit governanceInternal audit reviewing escalation timeliness and sign-off

Data inputs

  • Limit definitions and hierarchy (entity, desk, book, counterparty) with tiers and thresholds.
  • Current utilisation and exposure/risk metrics from the limit engines.
  • Prior-day utilisation and limit values for change decomposition.
  • Position and trade activity for active/passive attribution.
  • Market data moves for passive breach attribution.
  • Escalation matrix and contact/ownership mapping.

Functional requirements

  • Consolidate breaches from all limit engines (VaR, sensitivity/Greeks, notional, PFE, stress, concentration) into a single prioritised queue keyed to the limit hierarchy.
  • Classify each breach as active (position-driven), passive (market-move or limit-recalibration driven) or technical (data quality).
  • Attribute the breach driver via decomposition of utilisation change into position delta, market moves and limit changes.
  • Prioritise breaches by severity (utilisation over 100%, tenor of breach) and by limit tier (soft/advisory vs. hard/regulatory).
  • Route escalation per the governance matrix with time-stamped acknowledgements and enforce sign-off SLAs.
  • Track remediation actions (reduce position, request temporary limit increase, hedge) to closure with status and aging.
  • Produce breach MI: breach counts, aging, repeat offenders, and escalation-timeliness metrics.

Non-functional

  • Near-real-time ingestion so intraday breaches surface promptly for triage.
  • Immutable audit trail of classification, escalation and sign-off for regulatory review.
  • Configurable escalation matrix and thresholds without code change.
  • Resilience to partial feed failures with clear data-quality flagging.

Success metrics

  • Median time-to-triage and time-to-escalate within the governance SLA.
  • Active-vs-passive classification accuracy validated against analyst review (> 95%).
  • Reduction in overdue / unsigned breaches versus baseline.
  • Zero missed hard/regulatory limit escalations.
  • Reduction in repeat breaches through driver-based remediation.
SOP

Standard operating procedure

Purpose

Detect limit breaches continuously, classify each as active, passive or technical, escalate per the governance matrix, and drive remediation to closure with an audit trail.

Frequency

Continuous intraday monitoring for hard/regulatory limits; structured triage cycles after each major risk refresh and a full end-of-day clear-down.

1

Ingest live and EOD breach events from VaR, Greeks, notional, PFE, stress and concentration engines into a single prioritised queue.

Owner: Market Risk Analyst

Control: Feed-completeness heartbeats; technical/data-quality breaches quarantined automatically.

2

Decompose utilisation change into position, market-move and limit-change drivers; classify each breach active, passive or technical.

Owner: Market Risk Analyst

Control: Attribution must sum to utilisation change within tolerance; misclassifications logged for QA.

3

For technical breaches, open a data-quality ticket and suppress desk escalation until feeds are clean.

Owner: Risk Technology / Ops

Control: Technical quarantine register with re-open when feed is remediated.

4

For active breaches, prioritise by severity and limit tier, assign the desk owner, and escalate per the governance matrix with SLA clocks.

Owner: Risk Manager

Control: Escalation SLA monitoring; time-stamped acknowledgement required.

5

Desk remediates (reduce, hedge, or request temporary limit increase); passive breaches are documented and monitored without forced de-risking unless policy requires.

Owner: Desk Head

Control: Remediation action log with aging; temporary increases need delegated-authority approval and expiry.

6

Close breaches only when utilisation is back within limit or an approved waiver is in force; publish breach MI.

Owner: Risk Manager

Control: Closure checklist and overdue MI sign-off to risk governance.

Escalation

Hard/regulatory breaches, unacknowledged items past SLA, or unresolved active excesses escalate up the limit hierarchy to Head of Market Risk and the CRO office; temporary limit increases require the correct delegated authority and are time-boxed with mandatory review.
