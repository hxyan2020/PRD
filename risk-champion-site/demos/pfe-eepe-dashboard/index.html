Problem

A dealer trades OTC derivatives under ISDA/CSA terms and must quantify potential future exposure and the exposure-at-default that drives counterparty capital, limits and CVA.

Approach

  1. Simulate correlated risk-factor paths (rates, FX) over the life of a netting set of swaps and forwards.
  2. Revalue the netting set along each path and build the exposure distribution at each time step.
  3. Extract PFE (high percentile), Expected Exposure and its profile, then Effective EPE and EAD = alpha × Effective EPE.
  4. Model CSA collateral and the margin period of risk to show how margining collapses the exposure profile.
  5. Add wrong-way risk and let the user flex leverage, collateral thresholds and MPOR interactively.

Deliverables & metrics

  • Monte-Carlo exposure engine with PFE/EE/EEPE/EAD
  • Collateralised vs. uncollateralised exposure comparison
  • Wrong-way-risk and margin-period-of-risk sensitivity views

Show in interview

  • PFE profile and peak exposure over time
  • Expected Exposure and Effective EPE
  • EAD collateralised vs. uncollateralised
  • Wrong-way-risk and MPOR sensitivity

Stack: Python · numpy / scipy · Monte Carlo · Interactive dashboard

Sample talk track

Uncollateralised, the netting set showed a large PFE hump around year two; adding a CSA with a modest threshold and a 10-day margin period of risk cut EAD by more than half — but wrong-way risk on the correlated name clawed some back. Seeing EE, EEPE and EAD together is exactly how you defend a counterparty limit and feed CVA.

PRD

Product requirements

Problem

Counterparty credit risk (CCR) managers lack a unified, drill-downable view of simulated exposure profiles across the netting-set universe. Potential Future Exposure (PFE) is used for limit management while Expected Exposure (EE) and Effective EPE (EEPE) drive regulatory capital, yet these are produced in disparate batches, are hard to reconcile against the SA-CCR EAD, and do not clearly reflect collateral, thresholds and the Margin Period of Risk (MPOR). This obscures limit utilisation, wrong-way risk and the impact of CSA terms on exposure.

Objective

Provide an interactive PFE/EE/EEPE dashboard driven by Monte Carlo exposure simulation that shows time-profiled exposure per counterparty and netting set, incorporates collateral and MPOR, computes EEPE for capital, and reconciles simulated exposure to the SA-CCR EAD used in RWA.

Users

Counterparty Credit Risk managers monitoring PFE limit utilisationCCR capital analysts computing EEPE and reconciling to SA-CCR EADCredit officers assessing counterparty appetite and CSA termsXVA / CVA desk consuming exposure profiles for pricingRisk technology and model validation reviewing the exposure engine

Data inputs

  • Trade population with product terms, maturities and netting-set/CSA mapping.
  • Calibrated market data and model parameters (volatilities, correlations, mean reversion) for risk-factor evolution.
  • CSA / collateral agreement terms (threshold, MTA, IA, eligible collateral, MPOR).
  • Current collateral balances and margin call status.
  • Counterparty static and credit ratings for wrong-way risk and limit context.
  • Counterparty limit structure and current utilisation.

Functional requirements

  • Simulate risk-factor paths (rates, FX, credit, equity, commodity) under a calibrated real-world/risk-neutral measure and revalue trades along each path and time grid.
  • Aggregate trade-level exposures to netting-set level applying close-out netting, then compute the PFE profile at the chosen confidence quantile (e.g., 95%/99%).
  • Compute Expected Exposure (EE), Expected Positive Exposure (EPE) and Effective EPE (EEPE) with the non-decreasing effective EE construction.
  • Model collateral under the CSA: thresholds, minimum transfer amount, independent amount and the Margin Period of Risk (MPOR) for margined vs. unmargined sets.
  • Reconcile simulated EEPE-derived exposure to the SA-CCR EAD (1.4 x (RC + PFE add-on)) and explain differences by driver.
  • Provide interactive drill-down by counterparty, netting set, trade and time bucket, with limit utilisation and breach highlighting.
  • Flag wrong-way risk where exposure is positively correlated with counterparty credit quality deterioration.

Non-functional

  • Simulation scalability to the full counterparty portfolio with sufficient paths for stable tail estimates (target Monte Carlo error within tolerance).
  • Dashboard responsiveness with sub-second drill-down on pre-aggregated exposure cubes.
  • Reproducibility via fixed random seeds and versioned model calibration for audit and validation.
  • Traceability of every displayed number to the underlying scenario set, netting rules and CSA parameters.

Success metrics

  • EEPE-to-SA-CCR EAD reconciliation explained to > 95% by identified drivers.
  • Monte Carlo tail (PFE) estimation error within the model tolerance across counterparties.
  • Exposure batch and dashboard refresh delivered within the daily SLA.
  • Reduction in limit-breach detection latency versus the legacy end-of-day report.
  • Zero material exposure understatements identified in model validation backtesting.
SOP

Standard operating procedure

Purpose

Produce Monte Carlo exposure paths, build EE profiles and Effective EPE, derive EAD for capital, and check PFE against counterparty limits on a governed daily cycle.

Frequency

Daily Monte Carlo exposure batch at start of day; continuous intraday PFE limit monitoring when trade and collateral feeds refresh.

1

Validate market-data calibration and generate Monte Carlo risk-factor paths; confirm path count and seed for reproducibility.

Owner: CCR Quant / Risk Analyst

Control: Calibration diagnostics vs. tolerance; run halted if vol/correlation checks fail.

2

Revalue trades along each path and time grid; aggregate to netting-set exposure using close-out netting.

Owner: CCR Risk Analyst

Control: Netting-set mapping reconciled to the legal agreements register before aggregation.

3

Apply CSA collateral, thresholds and MPOR; construct the EE time profile and Effective EPE (non-decreasing EE).

Owner: CCR Risk Analyst

Control: MPOR assignment check for margined vs. unmargined sets; EEPE monotonicity assertion.

4

Convert Effective EPE to regulatory EAD and reconcile to SA-CCR EAD (1.4 × (RC + PFE add-on)); document material drivers.

Owner: CCR Capital Analyst

Control: EAD reconciliation pack with driver commentary above materiality.

5

Compute PFE at the policy quantile and compare utilisation to counterparty limits; refresh the dashboard.

Owner: CCR Manager

Control: Limit utilisation review with breach acknowledgement log.

6

Notify credit officers and XVA of limit pressure, EE profile shifts and wrong-way-risk flags.

Owner: CCR Manager

Control: Distribution log and sign-off on material-change notifications.

Escalation

PFE limit breaches, failed calibration, EEPE/EAD reconciliation breaks, or emerging wrong-way risk escalate to Head of Counterparty Credit Risk and the responsible credit officer; capital-impacting EAD breaks escalate to CCR capital lead and the CRO with a remediation timeline.
