Crypto Buy-Side

Digital Assets — Buy-Side Risk

Managing market, liquidation, counterparty and on-chain risk for crypto funds, treasuries and DeFi allocators.

Crypto buy-side risk management applies institutional risk discipline to a market that trades 24/7, settles on public blockchains, and blends familiar market risk with entirely new failure modes. The players are crypto-native hedge funds, venture and liquid token funds, corporate and DAO treasuries, and increasingly traditional asset managers gaining exposure via spot ETFs and structured products. The mandate is the same as any buy-side risk function — protect capital within a risk budget — but the instruments, venues and settlement rails are radically different.

The risk taxonomy expands well beyond price volatility. Perpetual futures and cross/isolated margin introduce liquidation-cascade risk where forced selling begets more forced selling. Centralised exchanges and lenders (CeFi) concentrate custody and counterparty risk — the 2022 collapses of Terra/LUNA, Three Arrows Capital, Celsius and FTX were, at heart, buy-side risk failures of leverage, concentration and counterparty due diligence. DeFi adds smart-contract, oracle, bridge and governance risk, while on-chain transparency simultaneously offers unprecedented real-time exposure and flow data. Stablecoin de-peg risk, staking and slashing, and MEV round out a uniquely broad surface.

In Singapore, digital-asset activity is regulated primarily under the Payment Services Act (PSA) for Digital Payment Token (DPT) services and, increasingly, the Financial Services and Markets Act (FSMA) framework, with MAS taking a deliberately investor-protection-led stance: restrictions on retail incentives and leverage, stablecoin issuance standards, and stringent DPT service-provider requirements on custody, AML/CFT and risk management. A credible crypto buy-side risk professional pairs on-chain fluency and quantitative rigour with a precise grasp of the MAS regime and the global patchwork (MiCA, US enforcement, Travel Rule).

Singapore

Key regulatory frameworks (MAS & statutes)

Payment Services Act (PSA) — Digital Payment Token (DPT) Services

Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) · In force (PSA 2019; strengthened via PS(Amendment) Act 2021, provisions phased in)

+

The PSA is the cornerstone of Singapore's crypto regime, licensing DPT service providers (dealing in, facilitating exchange of, transferring and custody of digital payment tokens). MAS applies AML/CFT, technology risk, custody and consumer-protection requirements, and has expanded the scope to cover custodial and transfer services.

Key requirements

  • Hold the appropriate PSA licence (e.g. Major Payment Institution) for DPT services and meet base-capital and fit-and-proper requirements.
  • Segregate customer assets and hold DPTs in a statutory trust with robust custody and cold-storage controls.
  • Implement AML/CFT programmes (aligned to MAS Notice PSN02) and the FATF Travel Rule for DPT transfers.
  • Comply with MAS restrictions limiting retail promotion, incentives and the provision of credit/leverage to retail DPT customers.

Implications for risk

  • Custody and segregation controls sit alongside market risk as first-order concerns for any fund holding assets on a provider.
  • Counterparty due diligence on exchanges/custodians maps directly to their PSA licensing status and controls.
  • Retail-leverage and promotion limits shape which venues and products are usable for Singapore-facing strategies.

References

  • · MAS — Payment Services Act 2019
  • · MAS Notice PSN02 — Prevention of Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (DPT service providers)
  • · MAS — Guidelines to discourage cryptocurrency trading by the general public

MAS Stablecoin Regulatory Framework (Single-Currency Stablecoins)

Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) · Finalised 2023 (implementing legislation in progress)

+

MAS finalised a framework for single-currency stablecoins (SCS) pegged to the SGD or a G10 currency, imposing reserve, capital, redemption and disclosure standards so that MAS-regulated stablecoins hold high-quality liquid reserves and offer par redemption.

Key requirements

  • Hold reserve assets of high quality and liquidity at least equal to par value, held with appropriate custody and segregation.
  • Guarantee redemption at par within a defined timeframe and provide clear disclosures and independent attestation of reserves.
  • Meet base-capital and solvency requirements to be recognised as a 'MAS-regulated stablecoin'.

Implications for risk

  • Provides a basis to differentiate lower-risk regulated stablecoins from unregulated/algorithmic ones in collateral and treasury policy.
  • De-peg and reserve-quality analysis becomes a monitored risk for any strategy holding stablecoins as cash equivalents.

References

  • · MAS — Response to Public Consultation on Proposed Regulatory Approach for Stablecoin-Related Activities (2023)

Financial Services and Markets Act (FSMA) — Digital Token Service Providers

Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) · Enacted 2022; DTSP provisions in force from 2025

+

The FSMA introduces an omnibus framework including licensing of Digital Token Service Providers (DTSPs) — notably Singapore-incorporated entities providing digital-token services outside Singapore — closing gaps and aligning with FATF standards on money-laundering and terrorism-financing risk.

Key requirements

  • Obtain a DTSP licence where in scope, including entities serving overseas clients from a Singapore base.
  • Meet AML/CFT, technology-risk and conduct standards under the FSMA framework.
  • Maintain adequate risk management and governance commensurate with the activity.

Implications for risk

  • Group structures with Singapore entities must map which arm holds which licence and the associated control expectations.
  • Extends the regulated perimeter, affecting counterparty selection and operational risk assessment.

References

  • · MAS — Financial Services and Markets Act 2022
  • · MAS — Guidelines / consultation on Digital Token Service Providers under the FSMA

MAS Technology Risk Management & Custody / Business Conduct Requirements for DPT

Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) · In force / phased (business-conduct measures from 2023-2024)

+

MAS applies its Technology Risk Management Guidelines and specific DPT business-conduct measures — segregation and custody of customer assets, restrictions on lending/staking of retail customer assets, complaints handling and risk disclosures — to raise consumer protection and operational resilience.

Key requirements

  • Segregate customer DPTs from the provider's own assets and hold them on trust with daily reconciliation.
  • Restrict use of retail customers' DPTs for lending or staking without adequate protections/consent.
  • Maintain technology, cyber, key-management and business-continuity controls aligned to the TRM Guidelines.

Implications for risk

  • Custody model (self-custody vs. qualified custodian vs. exchange) becomes a core risk decision with regulatory weight.
  • Rehypothecation and lending of assets is a monitored counterparty and operational risk.

References

  • · MAS — Technology Risk Management Guidelines
  • · MAS — Business conduct and consumer access measures for DPT service providers (2023)

World

Global frameworks market participants abide by

EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA)

European Union / ESMA / EBA · In force (phased application 2024-2025)

+

MiCA is the first comprehensive crypto framework in a major jurisdiction, covering issuers of asset-referenced and e-money tokens, crypto-asset service providers (CASPs), market-abuse rules, and reserve/redemption standards for stablecoins.

Key requirements

  • CASP authorisation with governance, custody, conflict-of-interest and prudential requirements.
  • Stablecoin (ART/EMT) issuers must hold segregated, high-quality reserves and honour redemption rights.
  • Market-abuse prohibitions (insider dealing, manipulation) extended to in-scope crypto-assets.

Implications for risk

  • Sets a benchmark for stablecoin reserve quality and CASP due diligence used globally in counterparty policy.
  • Market-abuse rules raise surveillance expectations relevant to trading strategies.

References

  • · EU — Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA)
  • · ESMA/EBA — MiCA technical standards

FATF Recommendations & the Travel Rule (VASPs)

Financial Action Task Force (FATF) · In force (Recommendation 15 / Travel Rule guidance updated)

+

FATF standards require Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs) to be licensed/registered and to transmit originator and beneficiary information with transfers above thresholds (the Travel Rule), forming the global baseline localised by MAS and others.

Key requirements

  • Conduct customer due diligence, sanctions screening and suspicious-transaction reporting.
  • Transmit required originator/beneficiary information for qualifying virtual-asset transfers.
  • Assess and mitigate ML/TF risks of new products, tokens and delivery channels.

Implications for risk

  • Financial-crime and sanctions risk are integral to counterparty and wallet screening for a crypto fund.
  • Travel Rule interoperability affects which counterparties and rails are usable.

References

  • · FATF — Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and VASPs
  • · FATF — Recommendation 15 (New Technologies)

US Regulatory & Enforcement Landscape (SEC / CFTC / Spot ETFs)

US SEC / CFTC · Evolving (spot BTC/ETH ETFs approved 2024; ongoing rulemaking and enforcement)

+

The US regulates crypto through a mix of securities and commodities law and active enforcement, while the 2024 approval of spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs opened regulated institutional access and reshaped market structure and flows.

Key requirements

  • Assess whether a token may be treated as a security (Howey analysis) with consequences for trading and disclosure.
  • Comply with CFTC oversight of crypto derivatives and anti-manipulation authority over commodities.
  • For ETF-related exposure, understand authorised-participant, custody and creation/redemption mechanics.

Implications for risk

  • Regulatory classification risk affects token universe, listing and delisting, and liquidity.
  • ETF flows introduce new demand/supply dynamics and basis-trade opportunities and risks.

References

  • · US SEC — Spot Bitcoin/Ether ETF approvals and related orders
  • · US CFTC — Digital asset commodity oversight and enforcement actions

Basel Committee — Prudential Treatment of Crypto-Asset Exposures

Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) · Finalised 2022 (implementation from 2025-2026)

+

The BCBS standard sets bank capital treatment for crypto exposures, splitting Group 1 (tokenised traditional assets and qualifying stablecoins) from Group 2 (unbacked cryptoassets carrying punitive capital), plus an exposure limit for Group 2.

Key requirements

  • Classify exposures into Group 1a/1b or Group 2a/2b with corresponding capital charges.
  • Apply a conservative capital treatment and exposure cap to unbacked crypto (Group 2).
  • Meet redemption-risk and reserve tests for a stablecoin to qualify for favourable treatment.

Implications for risk

  • Shapes how bank counterparties price and limit crypto activity, affecting liquidity and financing for funds.
  • Reinforces the reserve-quality distinction central to stablecoin risk assessment.

References

  • · BCBS — Prudential treatment of cryptoasset exposures (2022)

Perpetual Futures Liquidation & Funding Analysis

Derivatives / Market Risk
Open demo →

What it is. Analytics for leveraged crypto positions on perpetual futures — modelling liquidation price, maintenance margin, funding-rate carry, and the cascade dynamics when many positions liquidate together.

When used. Sizing leveraged positions, monitoring liquidation buffers, forecasting funding costs, and stress-testing cascade risk around volatile events.

Methodology. Compute liquidation price from entry, leverage and maintenance margin under cross vs. isolated margin; project funding P&L from funding-rate history; and model cascades by simulating price moves that trip clusters of liquidation levels, feeding forced selling back into price.

Inputs / outputs. Inputs: position size, leverage, margin mode, maintenance margin, funding rates, open-interest and liquidation-level data. Outputs: liquidation buffer, funding carry, and cascade loss under stress.

Interview talking points

  • Contrast cross vs. isolated margin and how each changes liquidation and contagion within a book.
  • Explain how funding rates signal positioning and cost of carry for a basis trade.
  • Describe the reflexive liquidation-cascade loop and why open interest clustering matters.

Skills: Derivatives mechanics · Python / data analysis · Market microstructure

Crypto Value-at-Risk & Volatility Modelling

VaRMarket Risk Measurement
Open demo →

What it is. Adaptation of VaR and expected shortfall to crypto's fat-tailed, high-volatility, 24/7 return profile, often using EWMA/GARCH volatility and higher-frequency data.

When used. Daily risk reporting for crypto funds, limit setting, and communicating tail risk to allocators used to TradFi metrics.

Methodology. Use historical simulation on 24/7 returns and Monte Carlo with fat-tailed distributions; apply EWMA/GARCH to capture volatility clustering; and stress with crypto-specific scenarios (LUNA, FTX, March-2020 liquidation).

Inputs / outputs. Inputs: 24/7 price returns, volatility estimates, correlations across tokens. Outputs: VaR/ES, volatility regime, and stress-scenario P&L.

Interview talking points

  • Explain why naive normal-distribution VaR badly understates crypto tail risk.
  • Discuss the challenge of correlation spikes to 1 across tokens in a crash.

Skills: Time-series / GARCH · Python · Tail statistics

Middle office

The middle office: flow, responsibilities & skills

In a crypto fund, the middle office is the 24/7 control layer between crypto PMs/traders and operations, custody and fund administration. It captures fills across centralised exchanges, OTC desks and on-chain venues, reconciles internal records against exchange APIs and the blockchain itself, values a portfolio that trades around the clock, and monitors counterparty, venue and liquidation risk. The post-FTX lesson is blunt: buy-side survival depends on an independent middle office that verifies where assets actually are and whether they can be withdrawn — not on the exchange's own dashboard.

Process flow (front → back office)

1

Middle office / Ops

Capture fills across CEX, OTC and DEX plus on-chain transfers, tag by wallet, venue and strategy, and normalise into a single book of record.

→↓

Reconciliations / MO

Reconcile balances and trades against exchange APIs and the blockchain (explorers, Fireblocks), matching on-chain hashes and clearing transfer breaks in near real time.

→↓
3

Independent valuation

Valuation / MO

Mark tokens against multiple venue prices, apply liquidity haircuts to thin/illiquid tokens, and run stablecoin peg and depeg monitoring.

→↓
4

Counterparty & venue exposure monitoring

Risk MO

Track CeFi exposure by venue, monitor withdrawal readiness and proof-of-reserves signals, and enforce venue concentration limits.

→↓
5

Margin & liquidation monitoring

Risk MO

Monitor perp/futures margin, distance-to-liquidation, funding and cross vs isolated margin exposure across venues.

→↓
6

NAV, compliance & investor reporting

MO / Fund admin

Strike NAV with a crypto-aware administrator, run Travel Rule and wallet screening (Chainalysis/Elliptic), and issue investor statements.

Core responsibilities

  • Capture and normalise trades and transfers across CEX, OTC and DeFi into one book of record.
  • Reconcile internal records against exchange APIs and on-chain balances continuously, not just at day-end.
  • Independently value tokens with liquidity haircuts and monitor stablecoin pegs.
  • Monitor counterparty/venue exposure, withdrawal readiness and proof-of-reserves.
  • Track perpetual/futures margin, funding and distance-to-liquidation across venues.
  • Support NAV strike, Travel Rule/AML screening and investor reporting with the administrator.
  • Maintain wallet inventory, whitelists and segregation between strategies and custodians.

Workflow

What a day looks like

Typical roles: Crypto Fund Risk Analyst / Manager · On-Chain / Quantitative Risk Analyst · Treasury & Counterparty Risk Officer (fund or DAO) · DeFi Portfolio Risk Specialist · Chief Risk Officer at a digital-asset manager

Morning

  • Review overnight 24/7 P&L and risk — the market never closed, so check moves across Asian, EU and US sessions.
  • Check liquidation buffers on leveraged/perpetual positions and funding-rate accruals.
  • Scan on-chain dashboards for large exchange inflows, reserve changes and whale/counterparty movements.
  • Confirm custody balances and reconcile positions across exchanges, custodians and on-chain wallets.

Midday

  • Assess new allocation or DeFi protocol proposals with the smart-contract/protocol risk scorecard.
  • Update counterparty exposure and per-venue limits; review proof-of-reserves and news for onboarded venues.
  • Run pre-trade risk for basis trades, options and leveraged positions with liquidation and funding analysis.
  • Monitor stablecoin pegs and reserve quality for treasury and collateral holdings.

Evening

  • Produce the daily risk pack — VaR/ES, concentration, liquidation buffers and counterparty exposure.
  • Recalibrate crypto-specific stress scenarios and volatility models after significant moves.
  • Document limit breaches and remediation; update the risk register with protocol and venue changes.
  • Coordinate with compliance on AML/sanctions screening and MAS/regulatory reporting.

Crisis mode

  • Stand up intraday monitoring during a crash, de-peg or venue-solvency event.
  • Model liquidation cascades and contagion across leveraged positions and correlated tokens.
  • Execute the de-risking playbook — withdraw from stressed venues, cut leverage, rotate to safer collateral.
  • Brief the CRO/partners and, where required, MAS, with real-time on-chain and exposure evidence.

Stakeholders: Portfolio managers / traders (front office) · Chief Risk Officer and investment committee · Compliance, legal and AML/sanctions teams · Custodians, exchanges, lenders and OTC desks · Blockchain/data and quant engineering teams · Investors, auditors and MAS / other regulators

Perpetual Futures Liquidation-Cascade Simulator

“I simulated how a single price shock triggers clustered liquidations that feed back into price — the mechanism behind every crypto flash-crash.”

A fund runs leveraged perpetual positions across venues. Risk must quantify liquidation buffers and model the reflexive cascade when many positions liquidate at once.

Approach

  1. Model a book of leveraged perpetual positions with cross vs. isolated margin and maintenance-margin rules.
  2. Compute per-position liquidation prices and aggregate liquidation levels by price band.
  3. Simulate a price shock that trips liquidation clusters and feed forced selling back into the price (reflexive loop).
  4. Layer funding-rate carry and open-interest concentration to capture positioning risk.
  5. Stress with historical analogues (May-2021, LUNA, FTX) and report cascade loss and survival leverage.

Stack: Python · numpy / pandas · Interactive charts · Exchange/on-chain data

Talk track: At 5x cross margin the book looked fine, but my cascade model showed that a 12% shock tripped a liquidation cluster whose forced selling drove another 8% — turning a manageable move into a 30% drawdown. That reflexivity is exactly why I capped strategy leverage at 3x and moved to isolated margin on the crowded pair.

  • Liquidation buffer (% to liquidation) per position
  • Cascade-amplified drawdown vs. initial shock
  • Open-interest concentration at key price bands
  • Max survivable leverage

DeFi Protocol Risk Scorecard & Yield-Sustainability Analyzer

“I turned DeFi due diligence into a repeatable scorecard — smart-contract, oracle, governance and tokenomics risk — with hard red-flag gates.”

Before allocating to a DeFi lending or yield protocol, risk must assess whether the advertised yield is sustainable and whether the protocol carries hidden smart-contract, oracle or governance risk.

Approach

  1. Define scoring dimensions: audit maturity, oracle design, TVL depth/concentration, admin-key/timelock governance, and yield sustainability.
  2. Pull on-chain TVL, oracle and governance parameters and combine with audit/bug-bounty history.
  3. Separate real yield (fees) from token-emission-subsidised yield and project decay.
  4. Apply red-flag gates (unaudited, single-key admin, manipulable oracle) that veto allocation regardless of score.
  5. Output a weighted score, position-size recommendation and monitoring triggers.

Stack: Python · On-chain data / APIs · Scorecard framework · Visualization

Talk track: One protocol showed a headline 24% APY, but my decomposition revealed 20 points came from token emissions decaying within months, and its oracle was a single spot-price feed vulnerable to flash-loan manipulation. That combination hit two red-flag gates, so it was a no regardless of the attractive score — exactly the kind of trap the scorecard exists to catch.

  • Composite protocol risk score by dimension
  • Real yield vs. emissions-subsidised yield
  • Admin-key / governance risk flags
  • Recommended position size and monitoring triggers

Stablecoin Depeg Early-Warning & Reserve Monitor

“I built a depeg monitor that watches peg deviation, DEX liquidity and reserve quality across stablecoins — the dashboard that would have flagged UST days before it broke.”

Funds hold treasury and collateral in stablecoins whose par value is assumed, not guaranteed. Risk must monitor peg stability, redemption capacity and reserve quality, and pre-define depeg playbooks.

Approach

  1. Ingest cross-venue prices and compute peg deviation, volatility and time-below-peg per stablecoin.
  2. Monitor on-chain liquidity depth (Curve/Uniswap pools) and redemption/mint activity as stress signals.
  3. Score reserve quality (cash vs T-bills vs commercial paper vs crypto-collateral) and attestation freshness.
  4. Build a composite depeg-risk score with tiered alert thresholds.
  5. Define automated de-risking playbooks (rotate, redeem, hedge) triggered by score bands.

Stack: Python · Web3 / DEX subgraph APIs · pandas · Plotly / dashboard

Talk track: My composite score blends peg deviation, DEX pool imbalance and reserve quality. In a backtest over the UST collapse, the pool-imbalance and accelerating redemption signals lit up amber well before the peg visibly broke — enough lead time to rotate treasury out. For fiat-backed coins the score is dominated by reserve composition and attestation age instead.

  • Peg deviation and time-below-peg
  • DEX liquidity depth / pool imbalance
  • Reserve-quality score & attestation age
  • Composite depeg-risk score vs alert bands

Crypto funds run cash-and-carry and funding-capture trades that look riskless but carry funding-flip, basis-blowout and venue risk. Risk must monitor the true risk-adjusted carry.

Approach

  1. Collect perpetual funding rates, futures basis and spot across venues in real time.
  2. Compute annualised carry for cash-and-carry and funding-capture strategies net of fees.
  3. Model funding-rate regime shifts and basis convergence/divergence risk.
  4. Overlay margin and distance-to-liquidation on the short-perp leg under a price spike.
  5. Produce a risk-adjusted carry metric and venue concentration view.

Stack: Python · Exchange APIs · pandas / numpy · Plotly

Talk track: The headline carry was ~12% annualised, but once I netted fees and stressed a funding flip plus a 20% spot spike squeezing the short-perp margin, the risk-adjusted carry halved and one venue held 60% of the exposure. That reframes 'free carry' as a funding-and-liquidation risk position that needs sizing and venue diversification.

On-Chain Proof-of-Reserves & Counterparty Exposure Tracker

“I built a tracker that verifies venue solvency signals and maps the fund's real, withdrawal-adjusted exposure to each CeFi counterparty — the discipline FTX made non-negotiable.”

Funds park assets on exchanges and lenders whose solvency they cannot see. Risk must independently track counterparty exposure, proof-of-reserves signals and withdrawal readiness.

Approach

  1. Aggregate the fund's balances and open positions per CeFi counterparty into a single exposure view.
  2. Ingest published proof-of-reserves / on-chain wallet clusters and monitor reserve trends and outflows.
  3. Track withdrawal-latency and processing signals as a real-time solvency proxy.
  4. Apply haircuts to exposure by counterparty risk tier and enforce concentration limits.
  5. Trigger staged de-risking (reduce, pre-withdraw, exit) as signals deteriorate.

Stack: Python · Web3 / explorer APIs · Exchange APIs · pandas · Plotly

Talk track: Rather than trust an exchange dashboard, I clustered its on-chain wallets and tracked reserve trends plus withdrawal latency as an independent solvency proxy. The fund's exposure was risk-tiered and haircut, with a hard concentration cap per venue. In a post-mortem replay of a failing lender, deteriorating outflows and slowing withdrawals would have tripped the exit trigger before the freeze.

  • Reserve trend / net outflow signal
  • Withdrawal-latency proxy
